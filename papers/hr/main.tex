\title{The Haldane--Rezayi zero modes are not a gapped ground-state manifold on the torus}
\author{A multi-agent AI team,\\ in collaboration with Barry Bradlyn}
\date{Preprint \quad---\quad 23 October 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We consider spin-$\tfrac12$ electrons in the lowest Landau level of a rectangular torus pierced by
$2N$ flux quanta, interacting through the hollow-core (pure $V_1$) pseudopotential. In the sector with
$N/2$ electrons of each spin the zero modes of this Hamiltonian form a ten-dimensional space, spanned by
the Haldane--Rezayi states on the torus; a proof of this fact is included. We prove that the eleventh eigenvalue of
the sector tends to zero as $N\to\infty$, at every fixed aspect ratio and uniformly over the rectangular tori whose
shorter side exceeds a fixed length: the zero modes are not separated from the rest of the spectrum by a gap that is
uniform in $N$. More generally, a patch of $p$ eigenvalues at the bottom of the sector whose width is less
than half of its gap to the rest, uniformly in $N$, has $p$ divisible by four. The proof is a lowest-Landau-level
version of the fractional index theorem of Bachmann, Bols, De Roeck and Fraas, applied to the magnetic
translation by one orbital, with the gap assumed only inside the sector. Its main new ingredient is the
torus analogue of the windowed-Fourier frame embedding of \cite{OAI26}, an isometric embedding of the lowest
Landau level into a two-dimensional lattice fermion system in which the hollow-core Hamiltonian is a rapidly
decaying interaction of uniform size and a Lieb--Robinson-type propagation bound with a polynomial light cone
holds with constants independent of $N$, and in which every Landau-gauge orbital is exactly supported in a
column of lattice sites. The result does not exclude a gapped phase whose
ground-state space strictly contains the zero modes, and it is asymptotic: the constants are not
explicit. At fixed circumference $L_y$, a regime complementary to the one above, a variational argument with spin
twists, also given, bounds the eleventh eigenvalue by $C(L_y)/N$ for every even $N$, with $C(L_y)$ explicit.
\end{abstract}
